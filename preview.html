🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

Let's work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer.

  1. Radiation is just energetic particles + photons. In space: trapped protons/electrons in the Van Allen belts, solar storm protons, cosmic heavy ions. Near accelerators: mixed protons/neutrons/pions + gammas. On planes: neutron secondaries.
  2. Three things happen in chips: TID = slow sunburn (charge trapped in oxide → threshold drifts, leakage). DDD = crystal bruises (atoms knocked out → sensors/optics degrade). SEE = lightning strike (one particle flips a bit, glitches, latches, or burns).
  3. Small transistors = easy to flip. 1 unit of LET in 1 µm silicon dumps ~10.4 fC. An old 180 nm gate needs ~18 fC to flip; a 7 nm FinFET needs ~0.37 fC. That is why your phone chip is TID-tough but SEE-fragile.
  4. Power chips lie. Silicon-carbide looks ultra-hard, yet a 1200 V device can burn at 500 V when a heavy ion hits. Rule: operate below 200 V or prove a safe area.
  5. Fixes that work: triple-vote (TMR), error-correcting memory + frequent scrubbing, watchdog reboots, current limiters, voltage derating, redundancy.
  6. Proof that it works (RHA): mission dose ×2 margin, no latch-up below LET 75, no burnout below LET 37, plus a measured orbit rate. This repo prints that verdict for you (EXP05).

❓ FAQ (simple answers)

Do I need a lab?

No. Learn fully from simulation. When you do test, bring the RHA table to the facility (TAMU/LBNL/PSI/CHARM) and they will know what to do.

Which file do I show my boss?

results/exp05_rha.csv + this page. One table = flight rationale.

Can I use COTS in space?

Yes, in LEO with mitigation (scrub, watchdog, current limiter) and eyes open on SEL. No, in GEO for critical functions without proof.

Why Docker?

Same numpy/matplotlib versions everywhere → same plots for reviewer, student, and CI.
